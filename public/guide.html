Welcome to AI Engagement

A single place to understand how your people are really doing — and to act on it. This guide explains the app in plain language and shows you exactly where to go for what you need.

Why

An annual engagement survey is one snapshot a year. People change faster than that. AI Engagement replaces the once-a-year event with a continuous read of how people are doing — and puts the tools to respond in the same place.

What

One connected system for planning (org, goals, weekly priorities), engagement (pulses, reviews, development, surveys), and insights (dashboards that turn it all into a clear signal of who's thriving and who may be at risk).

How

Navigate from the left sidebar, grouped into Planning, Engagement, Documents, Insights, and System. What you see depends on your role. Open this Guide any time from the top-right icon.

The Big Idea

From a yearly survey to a living signal

The concept at the heart of the product: stop measuring engagement once a year, and start reading two things continuously — whether people are thriving, and whether anyone is a flight-risk.

Why

A single annual survey captures a mood on one day and calls it the year. A quiet decline can hide for months. The questions worth answering continuously are sharper: Are people thriving? and Are we about to lose someone we can't afford to?

What

Two lenses. Thriving is whether a person is in a good place — energized, growing, doing meaningful work. Flight-risk is the likelihood someone leaves, weighted by how much their loss would cost the team.

How

Every part of the app feeds these two lenses — check-ins, reviews, goals, and surveys — and the Insights area rolls them up so leaders see direction and trend, not just a number.

Thriving

Are people growing, supported, and doing work that matters — sustainably? Thriving is shown as a trend and direction (up, steady, or "needs a conversation"), never a loud alarm. It's a supportive read, not a scoreboard.

Flight-risk

Who might be heading for the door, and how much would it hurt to lose them? Surfacing this early — while there's still time for a conversation — is the whole point. Sensitive by design, so it's tightly access-controlled (see Roles & Privacy).

Performance is a lever, not the gauge. Strong performance helps people thrive, but it's a poor way to measure thriving — a burning-out star can look great on paper. So performance drives the action; pulses and surveys drive the read.

Planning

Planning

Where your organization, its goals, and everyone's weekly priorities live. This is the structure the rest of the app reads from.

Why

Thriving doesn't happen in the abstract — it happens against real goals and a real team structure. Planning connects who reports to whom, what we're trying to achieve, and what each person is focused on now.

What

Four surfaces: Organization (the org chart + talent views), OKRs (goals), OKR Analytics (goal roll-ups), and Weekly Plan (personal priorities).

How

Find them under Planning in the sidebar. Everyone can see OKRs, OKR Analytics, and Weekly Plan. Organization is for managers and above.

Organization & 9-Box Manager+Live›

What it is. An interactive org chart. Click a person to open their card, with tabs for their priorities, OKRs, engagement, assessments, and review history. Scope controls let managers view an individual, their direct reports, or a whole team.

The 9-box. A 3×3 talent grid that plots people on performance × potential. Each manager places their own reports; the app rolls every manager's placements into one org-wide board, and leaders can view it by cohort, department, or organization-wide.

How to use it

  1. Open Organization from the sidebar; browse the tree or search for a person.
  2. Click a person to open their card and switch tabs (Priorities, OKRs, Engagement, Assessments, Review, 9 Box).
  3. Use the scope control to move between an individual, your directs, and your team.
  4. On the 9-box, drag or select to place each report on performance × potential.
OKRs Everyone · view only below managerLive›

What it is. Objectives and their key results, organized into annual goal periods. Objectives can carry weights and owners and ladder up toward company goals. Past periods stay read-only so history is preserved.

How to use it

  1. Open OKRs and choose the active period.
  2. Create an objective, then add its key results with owners and weights.
  3. Update progress over the cycle; link priorities in Weekly Plan to the objective they serve.
OKR Analytics EveryoneLive›

What it is. A scorecard for a goal period: company attainment %, a team leaderboard, and a met / partial / missed breakdown, with an AI-written recap. It answers "how did we do against our goals?" at a glance.

How to use it

  1. Open OKR Analytics (managers and above) and pick the period.
  2. Read the attainment summary and leaderboard; expand a team to see its objectives.
Weekly Plan EveryoneLive›

What it is. Your personal weekly priorities. Priorities a manager assigns appear here read-only and attributed to them. Completed items archive by week, and unfinished ones can carry forward to next week.

How to use it

  1. Open Weekly Plan at the start of your week.
  2. Add your priorities; link any that support an OKR.
  3. Check items off as you go; carry forward what's unfinished into the new week.
Engagement

Engagement

The ongoing, two-way conversation with your people — the light-touch pulses, the structured reviews, the development tools, and the deeper periodic survey.

Why

The signal of how people are doing comes from regular, low-friction contact — not one big questionnaire. This area is where that contact happens and gets captured.

What

Four surfaces: Pulses (quick weekly check-ins), Reviews (the 1:1 hub, plus manager & peer reviews), Development (coaching, PIPs, exit surveys), and the Engagement Survey (the deep periodic read).

How

All four sit under Engagement in the sidebar. Reviews and Development each open a hub with sub-tabs. Individual responses stay private; managers see aggregates (see Roles & Privacy).

Explore each area
Pulses EveryoneLive›

What it is. Short, regular check-ins — a light read on how the week went, wins, and blockers. The question set is configurable, and responses roll up so the organization can see morale trends without anyone writing a report.

How to use it

  1. Open Pulses when prompted for your check-in.
  2. Answer the short question set — a minute or two, not a survey.
  3. Managers review team-level trends over time; individual answers stay yours.
Reviews — the 1:1 hub EveryoneLive›

What it is. The workbench for the manager↔employee relationship. It has three sub-tabs:

  • Reviews — the review workbench: values feedback, performance scoring, a summary, talking points, action items, and both shared and private notes.
  • Manager Review — a short instrument for rating a manager (1–5, with an anonymous option and optional comments).
  • Peer Review — the same shape for peers, with respondent pickers — the path toward full 360° feedback.

How to use it

  1. Open Reviews and pick the sub-tab you need. If you receive reviews rather than write them, the tab lists what you have been given — open one to see each question and how it was answered.
  2. If you write reviews: score values and performance, capture talking points, and note action items (shared items appear on the employee's Weekly Plan). Manager Review runs the same way in reverse — a user fills one in about their manager, and a manager reads the ones written about them.
  3. Every review produces a coaching plan — see Development.
Development — Coaching, PIP & Exit Everyone · created by Manager+Live›

What it is. The tools for growing people and handling the harder moments. Three sub-tabs:

  • Coaching Plans — 1–3 focus areas with an AI-drafted summary and PDF export. Every review produces one; a coaching plan can graduate into a PIP.
  • PIP — a formal Performance Improvement Plan: concerns, goals, supports, check-ins, and employee sign-off. (Required in some regions such as the UK; elsewhere accumulated written feedback plays the same role.)
  • Exit Survey — a coordinated exit diagnostic: the employee answers one part and the manager another, revealing the "surprise gap" — did the manager see the departure coming?

How to use it

  1. Open Development and choose Coaching Plans, PIP, or Exit Survey.
  2. For coaching, set focus areas and let the AI draft the summary; refine and export.
  3. For a PIP, capture concerns, expected outcomes, and supports; the employee signs off.
Engagement Survey Everyone takes it · results Manager+ · run by ELT · HRLive›

What it is. The deeper periodic survey, built to match the instrument you already know — a large question bank chosen per cycle, an eNPS score, and a full analytics suite (summary, engagement, drivers, statements, heatmap, eNPS, and feedback). Up to two years of prior survey history can be imported for trends.

How to use it

  1. ELT or HR builds a campaign, selecting questions from the bank for this cycle. No one else can start, edit, or send a survey — not even a sysadmin.
  2. Employees complete the survey; responses are anonymous within group thresholds.
  3. Review results across the analytics tabs; managers see aggregates for their teams, never individual answers.
i

Imported history carries favorability and participation trends, but not eNPS or free-text — those come only from surveys run inside the app.

Insights

Insights

Where everything comes together into a clear read. The dashboards turn planning and engagement activity into the two signals that matter: thriving and flight-risk.

Why

Raw activity isn't insight. Managers and leaders need the story — who needs a conversation, which teams are trending down, where attention pays off — without digging through every screen.

What

The Insights Dashboard, with a Manager Brief (at-a-glance check-ins, reviews, and activity) and Manager Effectiveness (how a manager is doing for their people).

How

Under Insights in the sidebar. Available to managers and above; the deeper you sit in leadership, the wider the view — always within the privacy rules.

Explore each area
Insights Dashboard Manager+Live›

What it is. Your command center as a manager — the roll-up of thriving and flight-risk for the people you're responsible for, with the trends and prompts that tell you where to spend attention this week.

How to use it

  1. Open Insights Dashboard (managers and above).
  2. Scan the summary for anyone trending toward "needs a conversation."
  3. Drill from a signal straight into that person's card or review.
!

Insights are only as real as the data behind them. Early on, with few completed check-ins and reviews, some panels will look sparse — they fill in as the team uses the app.

Manager Brief Manager+Live›

What it is. A single digest for a manager: recent check-ins, upcoming and completed reviews, and an activity feed for the team — the "what should I know right now" view.

How to use it

  1. Open Insights → Manager Brief.
  2. Use it to prep for 1:1s and catch anything that's slipped.
Manager Effectiveness Manager+Live›

What it is. A read on how a manager is doing for their people — drawing on manager-review feedback and team signals. Because people often leave managers before they leave companies, this is one of the earliest and most useful lenses.

How to use it

  1. Open Insights → Manager Effectiveness.
  2. Read it as a growth tool; raw upward feedback is aggregated to protect anonymity.
Data & Setup

Data & Setup

The foundation. Everything above only works when the people, structure, and reference data underneath are right. This is where admins set that up and keep it clean.

Why

Org charts, manager views, reviews, and every insight all read from one place. Get the directory and reporting lines right and the whole app comes alive; get them wrong and nothing downstream is trustworthy.

What

Documents / Overview (a map of the app's areas), Core Data (employees, job titles, departments, values, question banks, periods, rating scales), and the Admin console.

How

Under Documents and System in the sidebar. Mostly for admins. Set reference data here rather than typing it free-form elsewhere.

Explore each area
Core Data Everyone · employees and instruments restrictedLive›

What it is. The admin hub for all reference and configuration data. It holds the Employees directory (name, email, role, manager, job title, department, active flag), plus Job Titles, Departments, Company Values, the question banks (pulse, survey, peer review), performance criteria, the rating scale, review/survey/OKR periods, and org data.

How to use it

  1. Open Core Data and start with Employees — set each person's manager, title, and department.
  2. Confirm Job Titles and Departments match your organization.
  3. Set Company Values, question banks, criteria, rating scale, and the current periods before running reviews or surveys.
i

Title and Department are independent — a title can exist in many departments, so you set each one separately on a person.

Documents / Overview EveryoneLive›

What it is. A visual map of the app's areas and how they relate — a quick orientation surface. (This guide is the narrative companion to that map.)

How to use it

  1. Open Documents → Overview for a one-look picture of the system.
Admin Console HR · ELT · SysadminLive›

What it is. The system controls — user management, email settings, notifications, backups, data export, release notes, and system jobs. This is where the app is operated and kept healthy.

How to use it

  1. Open Admin (System group) to manage users, configure email, and run exports/backups.
  2. Use it for everyday operations: add a user, reset a password, adjust settings.
Roles & Privacy

Who sees what

This app holds some of the most sensitive information a company keeps. Trust is a feature. Here's exactly how visibility works — so everyone knows what's private and what isn't.

Why

People answer honestly only when they trust where their words go. Clear, enforced boundaries aren't red tape — they're what makes the signal real.

What

Everyone has one access level, and that plus your place in the org chart decides what you see. A manager sees their own branch — themselves and everyone who rolls up to them, all the way down. Individual engagement responses are protected; the most sensitive signals are limited to HR and skip-level leaders.

How

The app shows one experience shaped by your role. If you hit something you can't see, you can request access and an admin decides.

The five access levels

User

Their own world — weekly plan, OKRs (read), pulses, the reviews they have been given, their 1:1 notes and action items. Takes the engagement survey; answers stay private. Receives coaching plans, PIPs and reviews but writes none of them. No Organization chart, no Insights.

Manager

Everything a user has, plus their own branch of the org chart — everyone who rolls up to them, at any depth, not just direct reports: reviews, coaching, PIPs, the 9-box, team engagement results, and the Insights dashboards. Reads all Core Data. Cannot edit the manager-review questions they are rated with.

HR

Org-wide visibility for people operations, survey administration, sensitive exit and at-risk signals, and the person records everyone else can only read. On an exit survey HR reads the comparison and fills in neither half.

ELT

Org-wide, the same reach as HR, and shares ownership of the engagement survey. The only level besides HR that can start, edit or send one.

Sysadmin

Full system administration — the employee roster, access settings, configuration, email, backups and the Admin console. Deliberately cannot run the engagement survey: sysadmin administers the system, it does not address the company.

Glossary

Key terms

The words you'll see throughout the app, in plain language.

Thriving

A continuous read of whether a person is in a good place — growing, supported, doing meaningful work sustainably.

Flight-risk

The likelihood someone leaves, weighted by how costly their loss would be — surfaced early so there's time to act.

Pulse

A short, regular check-in on how the week went. The light-touch replacement for the big annual survey.

Weekly Plan

Your personal weekly priorities. Manager-assigned items appear here attributed to them.

OKR

Objective and its Key Results — a goal and the measurable outcomes that define success, organized by period.

Period

An annual cycle for goals, reviews, or surveys (e.g. "2026 Goals"). Past periods are read-only.

9-Box

A 3×3 talent grid plotting people on performance × potential; managers place reports, the app rolls them into one board.

Review

A structured manager↔employee evaluation — values, performance, a summary, and a coaching plan, in one place.

Coaching Plan

1–3 growth focus areas with an AI-drafted summary. Every review produces one; it can graduate into a PIP.

PIP

Performance Improvement Plan — a formal plan of concerns, goals, supports, and check-ins that the employee signs.

Exit Survey

A coordinated departure diagnostic answered by both employee and manager, revealing the "surprise gap."

Surprise Gap

The difference between how much a departure surprised the manager versus the employee — an early-warning signal.

eNPS

Employee Net Promoter Score — how likely people are to recommend working here. From surveys run in the app.

Manager Effectiveness

A read on how well a manager supports their people, drawn from upward feedback and team signals.

Aggregate

Results combined across a group so no individual can be identified — how managers see engagement data.

Core Data

The admin area that owns all reference data: employees, titles, departments, values, questions, periods, and scales.
